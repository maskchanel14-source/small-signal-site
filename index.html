The 12 points

Where a one-person business runs on memory

Read your current reality in the 0–3 anchors. The gap between 2 and 3 is usually one automation plus one habit, and it is the gap that frees your calendar.

  1. INTAKE

    Qualification before reply

    You answer every enquiry like a maybe-client, then discover the budget in week two.

    • Every enquiry gets the same enthusiastic reply
    • You ask budget and timeline, but by memory, in chat, inconsistently
    • One form or scripted 5 questions every enquiry must answer before you quote
    • Form scores fit automatically; anything under the cut-off gets a polite no or a referral, written by AI, approved by you

    45-minute fix: Write the 5 non-negotiable questions (scope, budget band, start date, decision-maker, what happens if we both say yes). Paste them into your enquiry auto-reply. Nothing else today.

  2. INTAKE

    Speed and shape of first reply

    Slow or unstructured replies. The prospect's second choice becomes their first.

    • Reply when you find the email, often 24-72 hours
    • You reply same-day when you're free
    • A reply template exists: outcome, next step, two time slots, price band
    • Template plus calendar link; enquiry answered same day even on a bad day; a weekly note tells you how many you sat on

    45-minute fix: Build the 5-line reply skeleton once and save it where you actually type (email signature, snippet tool, Notion button): acknowledgement, one clarifying question, price band, two slots, next step.

  3. SCOPE

    Proposal that pre-answers the argument

    Proposals turn into three weeks of 'can you just also...' negotiation.

    • A quote with a number and a paragraph
    • A template with deliverables and timeline
    • Template lists out-of-scope items, assumptions, revision count, what voids the price
    • The template is filled from the intake answers, reads itself back to the client in plain English, and every sent version is archived

    45-minute fix: Add an 'Not included' block with 6 bullets to your current proposal. Six bullets, twenty minutes, most scope creep dies there.

  4. SCOPE

    Agreement and deposit enforced before work

    You start before paper is signed, then invoice into a silence.

    • No contract, or a contract sent after work starts
    • A contract exists, sent and chased manually
    • Contract plus deposit is a gate: no kickoff until both land
    • Gate automated with a signed copy and deposit status visible in one place, and renewal terms stated up front

    45-minute fix: Make the deposit a hard line in your proposal's last section: 'Work starts when the signed agreement and the [X]% deposit land.' Then say it out loud once, on your next call.

  5. DELIVERY

    Kickoff packet and one source of truth

    Week one is chaos; the project lives across email, chat and three folders.

    • We just start
    • A welcome email with a couple of links
    • A fixed kickoff packet: what we do in week 1, what I need from you, where truth lives, when updates come
    • Packet sent automatically, client completes their half through a form, and the project has one folder and one doc of record

    45-minute fix: Write the 10-line kickoff note you wish every client got on day one: access list, decision-maker, response expectation, where files live, when the first update arrives.

  6. DELIVERY

    Update cadence the client never has to ask for

    Clients chase you. Chasing clients make them anxious and anxious clients add scope.

    • Updates when asked
    • Ad-hoc Friday notes
    • Fixed weekly digest: done, next, blocked, decision needed
    • Digest auto-drafted from your week's notes or tasks; sent on schedule even when you're underwater

    45-minute fix: Set a recurring 15-minute calendar block named 'client digests' and send this Friday's digest to every active client, thin version counts.

  7. DELIVERY

    Change and revision control with a price

    'One small tweak' six times, unbilled, invisible.

    • Revisions happen by goodwill
    • You say the revision count out loud
    • Change log exists: every request, in or out of scope, and its cost
    • Changes logged, priced and approved in writing before work; out-of-scope handled by a rate card, not a mood

    45-minute fix: Add a 'Change request' line to your tracker with 3 statuses: included, billable, discussed. From tomorrow, no change happens without one of those three words against it.

  8. DELIVERY

    Triage: interruptions do not own the day

    You work in the client's order of urgency, not yours, and ship nothing deep.

    • Everything is answered on arrival
    • Two fixed windows a day for messages
    • A triage rule: every inbound becomes do-now, schedule, delegate-to-AI, or archive
    • Inbox is a queue with an owner (the system, not you), AI drafts the routine 70%, you approve in one pass

    45-minute fix: Define the three labels you'll use for any inbound today (Now / Scheduled / Waiting-on-client) and apply them for one day. That is the whole quick win.

  9. MONEY

    Invoice, receipt and the 3-touch chase

    You invoice when you remember and chase when it's already awkward.

    • Invoices from a docs template, sent whenever
    • Numbered, dated, sent on completion
    • Trigger-based: milestone or Friday, with terms and payment link in the body
    • Auto-scheduled with a 3-touch sequence at day 1, 7, 14, escalation rule and a late-fee line that actually exists

    45-minute fix: Write the three chase emails now and save them as snippets: day 1 gentle ('sending this back to the top'), day 7 direct ('payment is 7 days late, here's what happens next'), day 14 formal ('work pauses on [date] until settled').

  10. MONEY

    Cash and margin visibility (not revenue)

    Revenue looks fine, the account is tight, and no project has a real margin number.

    • No view beyond the bank balance
    • A monthly income figure
    • A 13-week cash view plus hours-per-project so you can see margin
    • Both maintained weekly, plus a per-client profitability habit that changes what you quote next

    45-minute fix: Open a sheet. One row per active project: quoted, deposit in, hours spent, hourly floor. The hour column is the one people skip and the one that fixes pricing.

  11. KNOWLEDGE

    A place that remembers so you don't have to

    You solve the same problem four times a year and re-solve it from scratch each time.

    • It's in my head
    • Notes exist, scattered
    • One searchable store: decisions made, snippets, boilerplates, lessons
    • New decisions land there automatically at end of week, and AI is allowed to read it as context before it drafts anything for you

    45-minute fix: Create one file per client called 'decisions'. Start it now with the three decisions from this month and one line each: what, why, date.

  12. GROWTH

    Offboarding that harvests the proof

    Projects end with a handshake. No testimonial, no case study, no referral ask.

    • Nothing happens at the end
    • You ask for a review sometimes
    • A fixed offboarding message: outcome recap, testimonial ask with a 3-question prompt, referral line
    • Every closed project produces a case study paragraph and a dated testimonial, filed next to the project

    45-minute fix: Draft the one message you'll send at every project end: recap of their outcome in numbers, three questions that make a testimonial easy to write, and a permission line to publish it.

Before you automate anything

Guardrails

  • AI may draft: Client comms first drafts, change classification, research briefs, meeting summaries, invoice chase wording, my own weekly review
  • AI may decide: Nothing about money, dates, scope or who becomes a client
  • AI must never touch: Bank logins, contracts without your read, health or legal conclusions, client credentials, anything you haven't verified against your own record
  • Before every launch: Read the AI output once out loud. If you would not sign it, do not send it.
